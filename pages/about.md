{{SITE}} publishes ten international news stories every day. Each one is a short, plainly written summary of reporting done by established news organisations, with every source named and linked so you can go read the original.

We started this because the daily news has become exhausting to follow honestly. Most people do not have time to read six outlets and work out where they disagree. That is the job we try to do: read widely, keep what is verified, say clearly what is not, and leave the reader better informed rather than more agitated.

## What we publish

Ten stories a day, spread deliberately across the world and across subjects — conflict and diplomacy, the economy, technology, science and medicine, sport, culture, the environment, and society. We do this so that a day's edition is not ten variations on the same argument.

We look for stories that carry something worth knowing: a decision that will affect people's lives, a genuine advance, a moment of human achievement or repair. We do not manufacture optimism. If a day's news is hard, we report it as it is and try to include what is being done about it.

## What we are not

We want to be precise about this, because it matters:

- **We are not a wire service.** We have no correspondents and no one in the field. Everything we publish is derived from the reporting of organisations that do have them.
- **We do not break news.** If you read something here first, check the source links before relying on it.
- **We are not neutral about facts.** Being non-partisan does not mean treating every claim as equally supported. Where the evidence points one way, we say so; where it genuinely does not, we say that instead.
- **We are not a substitute for the outlets we summarise.** Journalism is expensive to produce. If a story matters to you, follow the link and support the newsroom that did the work.
- **We are not a newsroom with a staff.** Articles are written by an AI system and held twelve hours for a human editor to read. Most editions are released by that editor; some publish when the window expires, and those say so on every article. Our [editorial standards](/editorial-standards/) page sets out exactly how to tell the difference.

## How we try to stay non-partisan

We describe what happened, who said what, and what is disputed. We attribute claims to the people making them rather than asserting them in our own voice. We avoid loaded adjectives, and we apply the same scepticism to official statements from every government.

Where a story is politically contested, we present the substance of the competing positions and the evidence behind each, and we resist the temptation to award a winner. Our [editorial standards](/editorial-standards/) page sets out the rules we hold ourselves to in detail, including our use of AI.

## How this is paid for

The site is funded by advertising, which is why you will see ad slots on these pages. Advertisers have no involvement in what we cover or how we cover it, no advance notice of stories, and no ability to remove or alter one. We do not publish sponsored posts, affiliate links, or paid placements dressed up as articles. If that ever changes, this page changes first and the disclosure will be on the article itself.

## Ownership, funding and independence

We have no political party affiliation, no corporate parent, no state funding, and no undisclosed investors. If we ever accept funding from any source that could reasonably be seen to affect our coverage, we will disclose it here before publishing anything further.
