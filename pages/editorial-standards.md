These are the rules we work to. They are public so you can hold us to them.

## AI disclosure — read this first

Every article on this site is **researched and written by an AI system.** A human
editor is given twelve hours to read each day's edition before it goes out. Most
editions are released by that editor. When the window expires without anyone
releasing it, the edition publishes anyway — and **says so, on every article in it.**

We think you are entitled to know this in the first paragraph rather than finding it
in a footnote, and entitled to know it *per article* rather than as a vague promise
about the site in general.

### How to tell which you are reading

Look at the line under any headline, and at the notice at the foot of the article.

| What it says | What that means |
| --- | --- |
| *Read and approved by the editor before publication* | A person read this specific article and released it. |
| *Published on the review timer, NOT read by an editor* | Nobody read it. It passed the automated checks below and the twelve-hour window ran out. |

An edition that nobody reviewed also carries a warning box at the top of the day's
page. We do not bury it, and we do not average the two states into a comfortable
phrase like "editorially overseen." Each article tells you exactly what happened to
it.

### What happens each morning

1. The system searches for reporting on eight beats from the past 48 hours.
2. Each story must be corroborated by **two independent, established news
   organisations.** Two outlets republishing the same wire copy count as one.
3. It drafts an original summary. It does not copy sentences from sources.
4. An automated gate runs, and any article failing a hard rule is **discarded rather
   than published.** The gate checks that:
   - at least two independent source domains are cited;
   - **every source URL is actually fetched** and returns a live page;
   - every quotation carries an attribution;
   - any story involving casualties or a developing situation declares what is not
     yet confirmed;
   - the article is not written in our own opinion voice, and does not use tabloid
     framing, unnamed-authority phrasing, medical overstatement, or health and
     financial advice;
   - no photograph appears without a photographer credit.
5. Whatever survives is put in front of the editor, together with a list of what was
   dropped and why.
6. The editor reads it and releases it — or the twelve hours run out and it goes
   without them.

If fewer than three stories survive the automated gate, nothing is published at all
that day.

### What the editor actually does

They read every story in the edition and check it against the standards on this page
— whether the headline matches the article, whether anyone is named who should not
be, whether contested claims are attributed rather than asserted, whether figures are
marked provisional, whether a science claim outruns its study. They can rewrite a
line, drop a story, or reject the whole edition.

What they do not do is re-report anything. They are not a second newsroom. They are a
person applying judgement to a machine's output before it reaches you, which is a
real and limited thing.

### The limits of all this

The checks protect you from fabricated sources, dead links, unattributed quotes,
single-source stories and false certainty about developing events. Those are
mechanical failures, and a machine can catch them.

They do not protect you from a plausible, well-sourced article that has quietly
misunderstood what its sources said. That is the characteristic AI failure —
confident, fluent, subtly wrong — and it is precisely what the human read is for. On
an article the editor did not read, that layer is simply absent. This is the honest
limit of what we do, and it is why the source list sits on every article instead of
being tucked away: on any given piece, you checking the sources may be the last line
of defence.

Some further commitments:

- The AI does not invent quotations. Every quotation is reproduced from a source we
  link to.
- No article is presented as the eyewitness work of a reporter, because none of it is.
- **A person handles every correction**, on reviewed and unreviewed articles alike.
  When you write to us about an error, a human being reads it, checks it, and fixes
  it. Publishing may be automated; accountability is not.

If we change the review arrangement — a longer window, a shorter one, or requiring
approval before anything publishes — this page changes before the first article
affected by it appears.

## Sourcing

- **Two independent sources minimum.** A story runs only when at least two independent, established news organisations have reported it. Two outlets republishing the same wire copy count as one source, not two.
- **Every source is named and linked.** No "reports suggest", no "sources say", no unattributed claims.
- **Primary sources where they exist.** For scientific and medical stories we cite the paper, the trial registry or the agency release itself, not only the coverage of it. For official figures we name the authority that issued them and when.
- **We do not source from social media posts, anonymous accounts, or aggregators.**

## Accuracy and uncertainty

- Figures from developing stories change. We say so, we timestamp them, and we name the authority behind each number.
- Where reliable sources disagree, we report the disagreement rather than picking the more dramatic figure. Many articles carry a "what is not yet confirmed" box; that box is the point, not a disclaimer.
- We distinguish confirmed facts from estimates, forecasts, and preliminary findings, and we label which is which.
- We do not state a scientific finding more strongly than its own authors do, and we do not report preliminary or non-peer-reviewed work as settled.

## Impartiality

- We attribute contested claims to whoever is making them instead of asserting them ourselves.
- We avoid loaded adjectives and emotive framing, including in headlines.
- We apply identical scepticism to official statements from every government, without exception.
- We do not endorse candidates, parties or policy positions, and we do not publish opinion columns or editorials.
- On genuinely contested questions we set out the substance of the competing positions and the evidence for each.

## Harm, privacy and dignity

- **We do not name private individuals** who are victims of crime, accident or disaster, or their families. Public officials and public figures acting in a public capacity are named normally.
- We do not publish graphic imagery, and we do not use photographs of identifiable people in distress.
- We report suicide, violence and disaster without method detail or gratuitous specifics.
- We take particular care with content involving children, and we do not identify minors in crime or accident coverage.
- We do not repeat unproven allegations against named individuals, however widely they are circulating.

## Images

Our articles carry stock photographs from Pexels or generated section graphics. **They are illustrative only.** A photograph next to an article was not taken at the event described and does not depict anyone involved in it. Every image carries that notice in its caption along with the photographer's credit. We consider the alternative — a real photograph of unrelated real people placed beside a news event as though it showed that event — to be a form of misleading the reader, so we label it every single time.

We do not use AI-generated images. If that ever changes, images produced that way will be labelled as AI-generated in the caption, and this paragraph will change first.

## Copyright

We publish original summaries, not copies. We quote briefly and with attribution for the purpose of news reporting and comment, we link to the original in every case, and we do not reproduce photographs belonging to the outlets we summarise. Rights holders can reach us through the [contact page](/contact/).

## Independence

Advertising is sold programmatically and advertisers have no influence over editorial content, no advance notice of stories, and no ability to have coverage removed or changed. We publish no sponsored content, no affiliate links, and no paid placements. See [about](/about/) for funding and ownership.

## Corrections

We correct errors openly and log them. See the [corrections policy](/corrections/).
